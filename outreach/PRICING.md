# Pricing

The frame matters more than the number. Get the frame wrong and no number works.

---

## The rule: never sell minutes

The moment the conversation is denominated in minutes of audio, the comparison
is Sarvam at roughly **₹2.70 a finished minute** and Bhashini at **~₹250/month**,
and that comparison is unwinnable — their marginal cost is lower, they are better
funded, and the buyer can call their API directly without us.

We are not selling rendered audio. We are selling a **deployment**: a system that
runs inside their perimeter, and with it the compliance path they would otherwise
have to build.

The right comparison is what the alternative actually costs them —
a DPDP-defensible architecture, a vendor risk assessment, a cross-border transfer
review, the legal opinion on whether voice recordings are biometric, and the
residual exposure against penalties reaching ₹250 crore. Against that, a
two-lakh pilot is a rounding error, and that is the point.

If someone insists on a per-minute number, that is diagnostic: they are buying
TTS, not a private agent. They are wedge 4, they should use Sarvam, and the call
should end kindly.

## The numbers

### Wedge 1 — Regulated enterprise

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Scoped on-prem pilot** | **₹50,000 – ₹2,00,000** |
| What it buys | One installed deployment, one use case, hands-on install, two to four weeks of iteration |
| What it does not buy | Integration into core systems, SLA, 24×7, custom model work |

Price toward ₹50k when you want the logo, the workflow lesson and the speed.
Toward ₹2L when they have a named budget line and a compliance deadline driving
it. Above that, only once a pilot has already run.

Post-pilot is a per-deployment annual licence, and **do not quote one before the
first pilot has taught you what support actually costs.** A number invented now
will be wrong in the direction that hurts.

### Wedge 2 — OEM / ISV

Per device or per deployment, and the shape depends entirely on their volume.
Do not quote on a first call — take their unit volume away and come back.

Anchor privately: the value is that they can ship an offline Indic agent at all,
against a BOM that is otherwise a cloud subscription per unit forever. A per-unit
licence that undercuts a few years of their cloud line-item is defensible in both
directions.

Non-negotiable: **permissive licences throughout mean nothing in our tree
complicates their redistribution.** That is a large part of what they are paying
for, and it exists because of the audit.

### Wedge 3 — Prosumer

**₹2,000–5,000 one-time**, or **₹500/month**.

Prefer the one-time price. Professionals with a confidentiality duty and no IT
department are buying an artifact they own, and a subscription contradicts the
pitch — a recurring charge implies a service, and there is no service, which is
the whole point.

### Wedge 4 — Ed-tech narration

Opportunistic cash only, never the strategy, never a discount that sets a
precedent. Quote it high enough that losing is fine.

## Principles

**Never free.** The first invoice is the hinge — it converts an interested person
into a customer, and it is the only real evidence that the problem is worth
money. A free pilot teaches you nothing except that people accept free things.
Small is fine. Zero is not.

**First deal small, deliberately.** The first pilot's purpose is to learn the
deployment workflow, not to make money. Under-price it if that gets it signed
this quarter, and say plainly that it is an introductory price so the number does
not become the anchor for everyone after.

**Do the work by hand.** No self-serve, no platform. You, on a call, installing
it. `plan.md` §7 is explicit about this, and it is also how the OEM and
enterprise deployment workflows get discovered — from the third one, productise
what repeated.

**Charge for the audit separately if asked.** If a prospect wants the licence
audit output and the spec sheet methodology as a standalone artifact, that is a
real deliverable and a signal of a serious buyer. Do not give away the thing they
just told you they value.

**Nothing recurring that we cannot support.** A monthly fee implies availability.
One person cannot promise 24×7 — price and word the agreement so it does not
imply it.

## What is not for sale yet

- **SLAs and 24×7 support.** One person. Say so; see objection 6 in
  `CALL-GUIDE.md`.
- **Ports to Windows or Linux.** Apple Silicon and MLX today. Note the
  requirement, never promise the port on a call.
- **Indic languages beyond Hindi.** Roadmap, needing a checkpoint that speaks
  them — not a config flag, and not a thing to sell forward.
- **Custom model training.** Off the table on an 18 GB machine; revisit only if
  the IndiaAI compute application lands.
